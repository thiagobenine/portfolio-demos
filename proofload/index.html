1Findings at a glance

The first run failed at 2.1x peak: the checkout database pool ran out of connections. The soak found a memory leak that killed a pod after 5 h 40 min, and the spike exposed an order-events consumer that could not keep up. The client's team fixed all three and a re-run on 22 Sep verified them: checkout-api now holds 12,600 RPS with p95 at 212 ms, and p95 first crosses the 300 ms target at 14,300 RPS, 3.4x peak.

2Scenarios, sized on your peak

A crane hook is rated for a working load limit (WLL) and proof tested at a multiple of it before it goes into service. We do the same to your API: your last real peak is the WLL, and the proof load is the multiple we agree with you. This sample used 4,200 RPS and 3x. Put in your own peak to see the plan it would produce.

RPS

Requests per second at the busiest moment of your last big event.

Proof multiple
Working load limit (your peak)WLL 4,200 RPS
Proof loadPROOF 12,600 RPS

At 12,600 RPS, every 10 ms a request holds a database connection keeps 126 connections busy on average (arrival rate × hold time). That is the first sum we check against your pool.

Constant, stepped

1x, 2x, then 3x held for 20 min: steady-state latency at each step and the knee between them.

Spike

A third of peak, then proof load in 30 s, held 5 min: autoscaling, pools and queues under a sudden jump.

Soak

Your peak held for 6 hours: memory leaks, connection churn and slow drift that a 20 minute run never shows.

Endpoint mix at proof load

Shares come from the client's access logs, so the test puts load where the real traffic does. Arrival rate is open: requests start on schedule even when earlier ones are slow, so slowness shows up as latency instead of quietly lowering the load.

    1

    Database connection pool exhaustion

    checkout-api to Postgres, all write endpoints

    Cost at proof load

    Stopped the first run at 2.1x. 4.2% of requests failed with HTTP 503 after the 2 s pool timeout; p99 of the rest reached 1,840 ms.

    6 pods × 20 connections gave a pool of 120. Each request held a connection for about 13 ms, so 8,800 RPS kept about 114 busy (8,800 × 0.013). At that utilisation the wait queue grows faster than it drains.

        2

        Memory leak in the price cache

        checkout-api in-process cache, found by the soak

        Cost at proof load

        One pod OOM-killed at 5 h 40 min. From hour 4, longer GC pauses pushed p99 from 141 ms to 1,200 ms.

        Prices were cached per session ID with no size limit and no expiry, so every new session added entries that were never evicted: about 71 MB per hour per pod against a 1 GiB limit.

        Fix, applied by the client

        Bounded LRU: 50,000 entries, 10 min TTL, hit rate 94% on the re-run.

        3

        Queue consumer prefetch limit

        order-events consumers on RabbitMQ, found by the spike

        Cost at proof load

        No API errors, but order confirmations fell behind: lag peaked at 2 min 34 s and took about 3 min to drain after the spike.

        Prefetch 1 and 4 consumers per pod on 3 pods meant 12 messages in flight. At 12 ms per message including the wait for the next delivery, that is about 1,000 msg/s. The spike published 1,512 msg/s.

        Fix, applied by the client

        Prefetch 20 and 8 consumers per pod: the next message is already buffered, so each takes 8 ms, about 3,000 msg/s in total.

        6How a test runs

        Six steps, from your peak figure to a gate in your pipeline. You keep control of the URL, the dates and the go signal for every run.

        1

        Scope

        You sendLast peak (RPS per endpoint, or an access-log sample), the staging URL, the event date and your pass criteria.

        We send backA test plan: proof multiple, scenarios, endpoint mix, mocks and the run dates.

        2

        Script and calibrate

        You provideTest accounts and a way to seed synthetic data on staging.

        We doWrite the scenarios, mock external services with their recorded latency, and run a calibration pass to prove the load generators are not the bottleneck.

        3

        Run

        You watchYour own dashboards during the runs, if you want to, with a direct line to us.

        We runConstant, spike and soak from load generators outside your network, and stop at once if your team asks.

        4

        Report

        You getThe written report: verdict per scenario, percentiles, errors, throughput, bottlenecks ranked by impact with their evidence.

        We walk throughEach finding with the engineers who will fix it, with the traces and metrics behind it.

        5

        Re-run

        You fixWhat the report found, on your schedule.

        We verifyBy running the failed scenarios again and adding the results to the same report.

        6

        Hand over the gate

        You keepThe trimmed scenario and its thresholds, wired into your CI.

        We tuneThe thresholds so the gate fails on real regressions, not on noise.

        What a Proofload test never touches

        • Production trafficTests run against staging or pre-production URLs you control and name in writing.
        • Third-party APIsPayments, email, SMS and partner APIs are mocked with their recorded latency, so no one else gets load and nothing is charged.
        • Customer dataStaging is seeded with synthetic carts, users and orders shaped like yours.

        Questions backend leads ask first

        Staging is smaller than production. Is the result still useful?
        Yes, if the report says how. We measure the ceiling per pod and what saturates first, and state the scaling assumption in writing. A small staging result is never multiplied into a production number without saying so.
        What can you test?
        HTTP APIs, REST or GraphQL, with authentication scripted per endpoint and data seeded to match your real carts, users and orders.
        Why not just run an open-source tool ourselves?
        You can, and the gate we hand over is a plain file you own. What the engagement adds is the sizing, the calibration, the reading of the results and the written ranking of what to fix first.
